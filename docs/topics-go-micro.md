# Five episodes from go.micro's playbook (2026-09-04)

Source: go.micro's 80 reels, ranked by plays, top six transcribed with beats.
22k followers, 79 posts, median 5.2k plays; the top six run 47 to 94s and pull
67k to 192k plays. What their winners share:

- Hook is a number or a date, stated flat. "One dose of psilocybin grew about
  10% more brain connections in just 24 hours." "In 2130 BC, someone in the
  Argentine Andes was already using DMT."
- Authority comes from naming the study: institution, journal, year, inside
  the first 15s. Yale, Cornell, PNAS, NASA, Emory and Baylor.
- One "Then researchers looked at..." turn, one "But..." caveat, then a
  philosophical kicker: "It grows out of the ground." "Who decided what it
  means?" "Still waiting, root and all, for someone to fund the proof."
- Slow: 125 to 160 words per minute (Johnny Harris runs 175 to 200). CGI
  neurons, molecules, glowing artifacts. Almost no on-screen text.
- The caption does the heavy lifting: "Here is what did not fit into the
  video", then sources. Comments trust them because the receipts are there.

What we take: the number-or-date hook, the named study by 15s, the caveat
beat, the caption-as-sources. What we leave: the slow read, the CGI, the
cosmic kicker. Ours end on a place and a person, per docs/script-playbook.md.

Topics below are 75 to 90s. Two are UK policy stories, one is the UK's native
species, two are the two biggest go.micro themes (neuroplasticity, deep time).

## 1. The Class A drug the NHS just prescribed (UK policy)

Pin: Maudsley Hospital, Denmark Hill, London. Person: Prof James Rucker,
King's College London. Receipt: Nature Medicine 2026 paper; a Home Office
Schedule 1 licence.

HOOK. "This is an NHS hospital in south London. Last year, sixty patients in it
were given a Class A drug on prescription. And it worked."
MAP whip to Denmark Hill. Label: MAUDSLEY HOSPITAL / London / *NIHR trial,
2026*.
"Sixty adults with depression that nothing else had touched. Half got a
single 25 milligram dose of psilocybin. Half got a placebo."
THE CATCH. "At three weeks, forty percent of the psilocybin group were in
remission. The placebo group? Three percent. One dose."
WHO. "The trial was led by James Rucker at King's College London, paid for by
the NIHR, the taxpayer, and run inside the NHS. Published in Nature Medicine
this year."
NOW, WHY DOES THAT MATTER. "Because in Britain, psilocybin is Schedule 1. The
legal definition of Schedule 1 is: no medical use. To even run this trial,
King's needed a Home Office licence that costs about five thousand pounds and
takes months. The mushroom in the study is the same one growing in every
sheep field in Wales."
WHERE IT STANDS. "In July 2025 the government agreed, in principle, to let
universities run this research without the licence. It is being piloted. No
date."
KICKER. "So the law says no medical use. The NHS's own trial says forty
percent remission. One of them is going to have to move."

## 2. The summer you could buy magic mushrooms in Camden (UK policy)

Pin: Camden Town, London. Person: the Home Office minister who signed
Section 21 (verify name from Hansard, 23 June 2005). Receipt: Hansard written
statement; a 2004 shop photo.

HOOK. "In 2004 you could walk into a shop in this market and buy magic
mushrooms. Legally. Over the counter. With a receipt."
MAP whip to Camden. Label: CAMDEN TOWN / London / *summer 2005*.
"Psilocybin had been Class A since 1971. But the law banned 'preparations'.
A dried mushroom was a preparation. A fresh one, still wet from the tub,
was legally just a mushroom."
THE CATCH. "So by 2004 there were more than four hundred shops across the UK
selling them fresh. Chilled cabinets. Price lists. Mexican, Colombian, Thai."
WHO. "The Home Office noticed. In the Drugs Act 2005, Section 21, one line
was added: any fungus containing psilocin is a Class A drug. Fresh, dried,
whatever."
ESCALATION. "It came into force on the eighteenth of July 2005. On the
seventeenth, selling them was a business. On the eighteenth it carried up to
life in prison for supply. Nothing about the mushroom changed."
NOW, WHY. "Because the government said open sale sent the wrong message. Not
because of overdoses. There are none on record."
KICKER. "Twenty years on, the mushroom that was in those chilled cabinets,
Psilocybe cubensis, is the one we've been tracing back to Africa. Same
species. Different postcode."

## 3. One dose, one month, ten percent more brain (go.micro's biggest theme)

Pin: Ithaca, New York (Cornell) with a flash to New Haven. Person: Alex Kwan.
Receipt: Neuron 2021 (Shao, Liao, Kwan); Cell 2026 (Jiang, Shao, Kwan).

HOOK. "This is a mouse neuron, photographed through a glass window in its
skull. Twenty-four hours earlier it was given one dose of psilocybin. Look
at what grew."
MAP whip to Ithaca. Label: KWAN LAB / Cornell / *Neuron 2021, Cell 2026*.
"Alex Kwan's lab imaged the same neurons before and after. Within a day, the
spines that let neurons talk to each other were ten percent more numerous
and ten percent bigger."
THE CATCH. "A month later, they were still there."
ESCALATION. "Then, this year, the lab asked where the rewiring was going. They
traced the inputs to those neurons across the whole mouse brain. It wasn't
random. Feedback loops in the cortex, the kind that loop on themselves, got
weaker. Pathways from perception down to action got stronger."
THE CAVEAT. "And here is the part nobody puts in the headline. When they
silenced one input region during the dose, the rewiring in that pathway
didn't happen. The drug doesn't rebuild the brain on its own. Whatever was
firing at the time is what gets built."
WHERE IT STANDS. "This is mice. Human scans show the network shift, not the
spines. Nobody has watched a human neuron grow."
KICKER. "So the trip might not be the medicine. What you're doing during it
might be."

## 4. Why the liberty cap makes it (UK's native species)

Pin: a sheep pasture in Wales (choose a real hillside for the label).
Species: Psilocybe semilanceata, the liberty cap. Receipt: "Wherefore the
magic?" Ecology and Evolution 2026 (Matthews Nicholass, Flis, Hanley, Knight,
Lane, Littlejohn, Thom; verify institution before recording).

HOOK. "This is the only magic mushroom that grows wild across Britain. And
until this year, nobody had run a single experiment on why it bothers."
MAP whip to the pasture. Label: LIBERTY CAP / Psilocybe semilanceata /
*Wales*.
"It grows on sheep fields from Devon to the Highlands. Psilocybin costs the
mushroom energy to make. So it must be doing something for the mushroom."
THE CATCH. "The first empirical test was published in April. Fruit fly larvae
raised on Psilocybe extract survived less, pupated less, and moved less.
Adults came out smaller, with lopsided wings. So: a defence."
ESCALATION. "Except the flies with no serotonin receptor, the one psilocybin
works on in us, reacted exactly the same. Whatever it does to a fly, it isn't
what it does to you."
MORE CRACKS. "And the insects living on wild liberty caps? Almost the same
crowd found on a mushroom that makes no psilocybin at all."
NOW, WHY. "One live theory is slugs. Psilocybe shows up in the genetic record
right after the asteroid that killed the dinosaurs, when fungi and land
slugs both exploded. Nobody has tested it. The authors say slugs are the
obvious next experiment."
KICKER. "So the best guess for why the trip exists is that a mushroom was
trying to poison a slug. And missed."

## 5. The oldest picture of a magic mushroom (deep time, go.micro's #2 theme)

Pin: Villar del Humo, Cuenca, Spain. Person: Brian Akers. Receipt: Economic
Botany 2011; the mural tracing.

HOOK. "This is a rock wall in central Spain. Someone painted it about six
thousand years ago. Count the things in the bottom row."
MAP whip to Cuenca. Label: SELVA PASCUALA / Villar del Humo, Spain / *c. 4000
BC*.
"Thirteen of them. Convex caps, thin wavy stems, no ring. Next to a bull."
THE CATCH. "In 2011 a mycologist called Brian Akers compared them to every
mushroom that grows in that valley today. One matched. Psilocybe hispanica.
A magic mushroom. It grows on dung a few miles from the wall."
ESCALATION. "That would make this the oldest picture of a psilocybin mushroom
in Europe. Older than Stonehenge. Older than writing."
THE CAVEAT. "It's an interpretation. Nobody can test the paint for
psilocybin. The argument is shape, location, and the fact that shamans
elsewhere painted exactly this."
WHERE IT STANDS. "Psilocybe hispanica wasn't even described by science until
the year 2000. The painting had been waiting six thousand years for someone
to name what it showed."
KICKER. "We keep calling this a discovery. The wall says otherwise."
