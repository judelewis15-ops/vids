# Script playbook (Johnny Harris method)

Built from his 8 best-performing reels of the last four months (transcripts,
beats and on-screen text pulled 2026-09-04; 235k to 2.8M plays each). Apply to
every Hypha Origins script.

## Length

| Reel | Plays | Length |
|---|---|---|
| How marriage became a bad deal for women | 2.8M | 170s |
| Only 4% of Americans get real elections | 1.4M | 129s |
| Cartel member found playing Padel | 972k | 94s |
| This prolific mathematician is a cat | 564k | 61s |
| This town has 1,000+ dentists | 464k | 71s |
| The low-tech solution to modern dating | 381k | 151s |
| Taiwan's backstory, explained | 351k | 94s |
| The FIFA scheme tearing football apart | 283k | 80s |

Nothing in the top eight is under 60s. One story, one place, one person: 60 to
100s. A data argument with several charts: 2 to 3 minutes. 45s is too short to
land a reveal AND explain it; write 75 to 95s and cut down only if it drags.

Pace: 155 to 200 words per minute, most around 180. On-screen text: 10 to 23
labels per reel, key phrases and names, never full captions. Voiceover over
b-roll for stories, talking head for data pieces.

## The hook (first 1 to 4 seconds)

One plain sentence that points at a thing on screen and contains a
contradiction. No "so", no "the craziest part is", no naming the series.

- "In Dubai, there's a Padel club that counts a wanted criminal as a valued member."
- "This is a Google Scholar page for someone named Larry Richardson."
- "And look at what happens when you zoom into this tiny little town."
- "Taiwan isn't just any island."
- "The King of FIFA just launched a new scheme to make money, and it involves Donald Trump."

Two shapes cover almost all of them: **"This is [thing on screen]."** and
**"In [place], there's a [thing] that [shouldn't be true]."** Starting
mid-sentence ("And look at...") works because it feels like a jump cut into a
story already running.

## The arc

1. **Hook** (0 to 4s). Point at the thing.
2. **Specific detail** (4 to 15s). A name, a poster, a number. "One of its
   promotional posters features a player named Ian Thomas."
3. **The catch** (15 to 25s). "But the catch is..." / "Except..." /
   "It turns out..." The reveal lands before 25s, always.
4. **Who and why it matters** (25 to 50s). Concrete facts with numbers as
   characters: "18 murders across four countries", "1,000 dentists, 5,500
   people", "132 citations".
5. **Escalation** (50 to 75s). He did X, then Y, then Z. Repeat a pattern to
   build rhythm ("Nope. It's the same again." four bar charts in a row).
6. **"Now, why?"** Ask the question out loud, then answer it in one breath.
   "Now, why is Infantino doing this? Because..."
7. **Where it stands today.** "In 2026, the cartel's boss was arrested..."
8. **Kicker.** A quote, a nickname, a one-line irony. "And now it goes by the
   nickname Molar City." Never "follow for more". A CTA only when pointing at
   a longer piece.

Every top reel has a **named person** (or cat) at the centre, a **place** you
can put on a map, and a **receipt** on screen (poster, paper, chart, Scholar
page, patent). If the script has none of these, it is not ready.

## Lingo

Short spoken connectives that make it feel live, not written:
"Boom." "Look at this." "Yeah, look at that." "Get this," "Now, check out..."
"So okay," "All right. Last one." "Nope." "It turns out" "the catch is"
"sort of" "basically" "literally". He asks a question and answers it. He
addresses you: "you've probably seen", "I've got some news for you".

Never: "deep dive", "rewrites history", "the craziest part is", stacked
adjectives, more than one number per sentence, jargon without a picture
(Polymorph A needs a crystal on screen the moment it is said).

## What this changes for Hypha Origins

- The series line ("This is episode one of Hypha Origins...") does not belong
  in the first 10 seconds. None of the eight reels announce themselves. Put
  "HYPHA ORIGINS · 02" as on-screen text on the map label card, and say the
  name once at the end if at all.
- The map whip stays: he uses zooming maps constantly. But the VO over it must
  be story ("made in a lab in Basel in 1958"), not an intro.
- Cartoon b-roll is fine, but every beat needs one receipt cut in: the paper,
  the patent, the vendor listing, the Google Scholar page. Receipts are what
  make it journalism instead of animation.
- Write 75 to 95s. Cut to 60 only if a beat is dead weight.
