# Mercenaries when they're Russian, contractors when they're American

Status: draft script, not yet recorded. Facts below still need checking.
Format: map-led reel in the style of Johnny Harris's Nile video (tilted 3D relief map, slow push-ins, one element added per beat, small captions), with our script structure (broad verdict, history, turn, close).
Target length: about 115 seconds, roughly 320 words. Haiti is the second half.

## Script

When Russia sends hired guns abroad, we call them mercenaries. When America does it, we call them contractors.

Start here. Wagner. A private army working for the Kremlin. Libya. The Central African Republic. Mali. They prop up governments, and take their pay in gold and mining rights. In one Malian village, Moura, hundreds of civilians were killed in five days.

And we're right to condemn it.

But go back twenty years. Baghdad, 2007. Nisour Square. Guards from an American company called Blackwater open fire in traffic. Fourteen civilians dead. Four guards were convicted. In 2020, the President pardoned all four.

The man who founded Blackwater is Erik Prince. And he's back in business. Here.

Seven hundred miles from Miami. Port-au-Prince. Up to ninety percent of it is run by gangs. The port. Every highway out. Then they went north, into the Artibonite, where Haiti grows its rice. A million and a half people have fled.

The government can't take it back. So it hired Prince's new company, Vectus Global, with a licence from the US State Department. They fly armed drones over one of the most crowded cities in the Americas.

Human Rights Watch counted a hundred and forty-one drone operations in ten months. One thousand, two hundred and forty-three dead. Seventeen of them were children.

But here's the part people miss. Nobody elected them. And nobody can vote them out.

It isn't the same as Wagner. Haiti's government asked for this, and the gangs are real. But if a private army killing civilians in someone else's country is wrong when Moscow sends it, we should be able to say what it is when Washington signs the licence.

## Alternative hooks

- "We spent years condemning Russia's private army. It's time to look at America's."
- "The man behind Blackwater is fighting a drone war seven hundred miles from Miami."
- "Twelve hundred people have been killed by a private company's drones. You probably haven't heard about it."

## Accuracy and fairness care

- The comparison is the argument, so the differences have to be stated, and the script states them: Wagner works for the Russian state; Vectus was contracted by Haiti's government and licensed by the US. Do not say or imply the US government ordered the strikes.
- Erik Prince sold Blackwater in 2010 and was not charged over Nisour Square. The script says he founded the company, nothing more.
- Attribute the Haiti death toll to Human Rights Watch by name on screen. The 1,243 figure is all deaths from the documented drone operations, most of them alleged gang members; 17 children and 43 adults with no gang ties are the civilians HRW identified. Do not caption it as "1,243 civilians".
- The gang-control fills must be traced from a published map (the UN Panel of Experts report of September 2026 includes one from June 2026), not drawn by eye.

## Facts to check before recording

- **Wagner.** Formally disbanded in 2023 and folded into the Africa Corps under Russian military intelligence; announced withdrawal from Mali in June 2025. The script uses the present tense for simplicity; consider "Wagner, now rebranded as the Africa Corps". Largest deployments: the Central African Republic, Libya and Mali (US Congressional Research Service). Mining concessions in the Central African Republic are from The Sentry; "gold" specifically is from memory.
- **Moura.** March 2022. One source in the search says more than 300 civilians killed; from memory, the UN human rights office later put it at 500 or more, and Malian forces led the operation with Wagner personnel taking part. "Hundreds" covers both; "five days" is from memory.
- **Nisour Square.** 16 September 2007. US prosecutors and most outlets say 14 civilians killed and 17 wounded; Iraqi authorities say 17 killed. Four guards convicted in 2014, pardoned on 22 December 2020. "Twenty years" is rounding 19.
- **Prince.** Founded Blackwater (1997, from memory) and Vectus Global.
- **Haiti.** Gang control "up to 90%" per the UN via Al Jazeera, 7 November 2026; an AP report says 70%. About 1.5 million displaced is from a secondary tracker citing the International Organization for Migration, June 2026. Vectus licence and contract are per JURIST and the Haitian Times. HRW: 141 operations, March 2025 to January 2026, at least 1,243 killed. CNN's tally of UN reporting counts 85 civilian deaths and over 2,000 strikes, so the sources count differently.
- **From memory, unconfirmed.** 700 miles from Miami; the Artibonite as Haiti's rice-growing region; "one of the most crowded cities in the Americas"; "nobody elected them" (check whether Haiti has held elections in 2026, and note the line is about the contractor, not the Haitian government).
