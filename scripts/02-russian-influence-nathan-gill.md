# That's not a bad apple. That's a shopping list.

Status: draft script, not yet recorded. Facts below still need checking.
Format: rebuttal reel (broad verdict, clip, "the problem is…", court facts, scale, turn, patriotic close).
Target length: about 100 seconds, roughly 300 words, about 20 cuts a minute.

## Script

Russia doesn't need to invade Britain. It just needs to rent a few of our politicians.

**[CLIP: Nigel Farage]** "Every single political party has a bad apple. In fact, many families finish up with a bad apple, these things happen."

His argument is essentially that this was one man, one mistake, nothing to see here.

The problem is, that's not what the court heard.

Nathan Gill sat in the European Parliament alongside Nigel Farage. He went on to lead Reform UK in Wales. And between 2018 and 2019, he took around forty thousand pounds in bribes. Eight counts. He pleaded guilty to every one.

And what was he paid for? Scripts. A pro-Russian politician wrote the words, and Gill stood up in the European Parliament and said them. Five thousand euros for a single TV interview.

The judge said the money traced back to Viktor Medvedchuk. Vladimir Putin is godfather to his daughter.

And oh, by the way, the judge said Gill enlisted other British MEPs to take the same line.

Ten and a half years.

But here's the part people miss. Forty thousand pounds. That's it. That's what it cost to put Moscow's words in a British politician's mouth. And our own Prime Minister says the Kremlin spends over a billion pounds a year on this. Bots. Fake websites. Forged logos of the BBC and British universities.

So that's not a bad apple. That's a shopping list. And Gill is just the one who got caught.

Criticising your country is democracy. Being paid by someone else's to do it is something else.

Britain has faced down far bigger threats than a troll farm. But we have to notice it first. Because loving your country means it isn't for sale. And the people who shout loudest about patriotism should be the first to say so.

## Legal and accuracy care

- Farage has denied knowing about Gill's conduct and has not been accused of any offence. The script does not say he knew, and nothing on screen should imply it.
- The judge said Gill "enlisted other British MEPs" to adopt the same line. Those MEPs were not charged. Do not name or picture identifiable individuals on that line.
- Keep reporting on donations to Reform or to Farage out of this video. It is a separate matter from the Gill case.

## Facts to check before recording

- **Farage quote.** Wording taken from a search summary of Press Association reporting, said on a Caerphilly campaign visit in October 2025. Confirm the exact words and find the footage.
- **Gill and Farage timeline.** The offences ran December 2018 to July 2019, when Gill was an MEP (UKIP, then the Brexit Party). He led Reform UK in Wales later, in 2021. The party and date details here are from memory; confirm them, and confirm "alongside Nigel Farage" is fair for that period.
- **Amounts.** The Crown Prosecution Service gave around £40,000. The €5,000 for a 112 Ukraine interview comes from press reports. Some outlets only say "thousands of euros".
- **Judge's remarks.** Voloshyn as architect, Medvedchuk as "ultimate source of the requests and the money", and "enlisted other British MEPs" are as reported by Spotlight on Corruption and others. Check against the published sentencing remarks.
- **Medvedchuk and Putin.** Putin being godfather to Medvedchuk's daughter is from memory, widely reported. Confirm.
- **£1.3bn and the forged branding.** Attributed to the Prime Minister's UN General Assembly speech in September 2026, but found only in secondary coverage, not an official transcript. The forged branding of 28 organisations was reportedly tied to interference around the 2019 general election. Check the speech text.
