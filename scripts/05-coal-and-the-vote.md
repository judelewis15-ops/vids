# A swamp decided how Britain votes

Status: draft script, not yet recorded. Facts below still need checking, and the central claim (the overlay) must be tested on real data before anything is animated.
Format: map-led reel in the style of Johnny Harris's Nile video. Voice-over on map for almost all of it.
Target length: about 85 seconds, roughly 210 words.
Build handoff: `handoff/coal-map-after-effects.md`.

## Script

Three hundred million years ago, a swamp decided how Britain would vote.

This is Britain. And this is what's underneath it. Coal. South Wales. Durham. Yorkshire. Nottinghamshire. Lancashire. The middle of Scotland.

Where there was coal, there were pits. By 1913, about three thousand of them, with over a million men underground.

Pits made villages. Villages made unions. And the unions built a political party.

This is the 2010 election. Labour's worst result since 1983. Take away the big cities, and look at what's left.

It's the same map.

Then the pits closed. The last deep mine shut in 2015. The vote stayed.

Until 2019. Bolsover, Labour since 1950. Sedgefield, Tony Blair's old seat. Gone.

In 2024, most of them came back. A year later, Durham, Nottinghamshire, Derbyshire and Lancashire handed their councils to Reform.

But here's the part people miss. These places didn't change sides three times in six years because they can't make up their minds. Coal gave them a reason to vote together. Nobody has given them another one.

The rock hasn't moved. The loyalty has. And whoever earns it next wins the country.

## Facts to check before recording

All of these are from memory.

- **Geology.** The Coal Measures are Carboniferous, roughly 300 to 320 million years old, formed from swamp forest.
- **1913.** Peak of the industry: over a million miners is well attested; "about three thousand" collieries needs a source.
- **2010.** Labour's vote share was its lowest since 1983. Using 2010 and 2019 matters for the build because both were fought on the same constituency boundaries.
- **Closures.** Kellingley, the last deep coal mine, closed in December 2015.
- **2019.** Bolsover had been Labour since its creation in 1950; Sedgefield was Tony Blair's seat. Both went Conservative.
- **2024.** Labour regained most of the seats it lost in 2019. Check both named seats specifically.
- **2025.** Reform UK won control of Durham, Nottinghamshire, Derbyshire and Lancashire county councils in the May local elections. Confirm each.
- **The exceptions.** Kent had a small coalfield and stayed Conservative. Big cities are Labour without coal. Nottinghamshire moved right earlier than other coalfields. The script handles cities on screen ("take away the big cities"); the others should stay visible, not be hidden.
- **The claim itself.** "It's the same map" has to survive the overlay test described in the handoff. If the match is weak, soften the line or pick a different election.
