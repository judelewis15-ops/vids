# At the table, or on the menu

Status: draft script, not yet recorded. Facts below still need checking.
Format: thesis reel (broad verdict, supporting clip, then versus now, what changed, turn, patriotic close).
Target length: about 115 seconds, roughly 340 words plus two clips, about 20 to 25 cuts a minute.

## Script

The world that kept Britain safe for eighty years is ending. And almost nobody in British politics will say it out loud.

**[CLIP: Mark Carney, Davos, January 2026]** "We are in the midst of a rupture, not a transition… Middle powers must act together, because if we're not at the table, we're on the menu."

That's Mark Carney. Prime Minister of Canada. He used to run the Bank of England. And he's right.

In 1941, Churchill and Roosevelt met on a warship off Newfoundland and wrote down what the world should look like after the war. No land grabs. Open trade. Free seas. That became the United Nations, NATO, and the trading system we still use.

And the deal was simple. America protects the system, and everybody else plays by the rules. For eighty years the US Navy kept the sea lanes open. Pirates, blockades, it didn't matter whose ship it was. And it worked. World trade grew more than forty times over.

So what changed? America did. It put tariffs on its own allies. And this year, American bombs started a war that all but shut the Strait of Hormuz. A fifth of the world's oil goes through it. Eighty-five ships a day became a trickle.

The navy that kept the world's waterways open helped close the most important one on the planet.

And our politicians? They know. They used to say so.

**[ON SCREEN: James Cleverly's 2015 tweet, 2 to 3 seconds]** "Dear Republican colleagues, please, please, please don't choose Donald Trump…"

Then he won. And they stopped.

**[CLIP: the dodge, 4 to 5 seconds. Candidate: Cleverly in 2024 refusing to say whether he would vote for Trump.]**

They're scared. Scared of the tariffs. Scared of the phone call. Canada shares a border with America, and its Prime Minister still said it out loud. What's our excuse?

But here's the part people miss. The middle powers aren't small. Put Britain, the European Union, Canada, Japan, Australia and South Korea together, and you've got an economy bigger than America's. Bigger than China's. They've just never had to act like it.

And it's already started. Canada has applied to join a defence force that Britain leads.

So no, the old order isn't coming back. But the things it stood for, open trade, borders that mean something, rules that apply to the big guy too, those weren't American ideas. A lot of them were ours.

Britain helped write the rules once, on a ship in the Atlantic. We can wait to find out what's on the menu. Or we can pull up a chair and write them again.

## Facts to check before recording

- **Carney quote.** Davos, 20 or 21 January 2026 (sources differ on the day). "We are in the midst of a rupture, not a transition" appears in the published transcripts. The menu line is reported as "if we're not at the table, we're on the menu" in the transcripts and "if you are not at the table, you are on the menu" by Al Jazeera. The two sentences are not adjacent in the speech, so the clip is two cuts; use the official transcript and footage.
- **Carney biography.** Governor of the Bank of England 2013 to 2020. From memory.
- **Atlantic Charter.** August 1941, Churchill and Roosevelt aboard ships in Placentia Bay, Newfoundland. Principles included no territorial aggrandisement, freer trade and freedom of the seas. From memory. It preceded and influenced the UN; saying it "became" the UN, NATO and the trading system is a simplification.
- **World trade growth.** The World Trade Organization has described world trade volume as roughly 40-plus times its 1950 level. From memory; confirm the current figure.
- **The tweet.** James Cleverly posted in December 2015: "Dear Republican colleagues, please, please, please don't choose Donald Trump as the front man for American Right-of-Centre politics. Please". The text is from a search result for the post on X; the date is inferred, so open the post, confirm it is still up and screenshot it.
- **The dodge clip.** This beat is about British politicians declining to criticise Trump in general, not about Iran. Candidates found: (1) Cleverly, July 2024, reported by HuffPost UK under the headline "James Cleverly Refuses To Say If He Would Vote For Trump"; the programme and exact exchange were not checked. In 2024 as Foreign Secretary he also told The House magazine that Trump's "particular style can be surprisingly effective". (2) Keir Starmer, November 2025, declining at Prime Minister's Questions to say whether he would urge Trump to drop his threat to sue the BBC, when asked by Ed Davey. (3) The Cleverly appearance on The Rest Is Politics: Leading (episode 193, 14 June 2026): no transcript was found and its listed topics do not mention Trump, so whether it contains a usable dodge is unconfirmed; listen to it before relying on it. Whichever is used, the question must be audible, and "scared" is your opinion of the motive, so say it to camera and do not caption it over their face.
- **The opening line may need softening.** Andy Burnham, asked by Laura Kuenssberg in July 2026 whether he would call out Trump if it was the right thing to do, said "of course". Whether he has since done so was not checked. If he has, "almost nobody in British politics will say it out loud" needs changing.
- **Starmer on Iran (earlier, longer version of this beat, kept as a fallback).** "Keir Starmer was Prime Minister when the bombs fell. To be fair, he kept us out of it. But he condemned Iran for hitting back, and he wouldn't say whether the strikes themselves were right or wrong. Then he let America use our bases. Carney had already said it out loud, a month earlier. Why can't we?" Supporting facts: He is no longer Prime Minister: he announced his resignation on 22 June 2026 and Andy Burnham took office on 20 July. The script says "was Prime Minister when the bombs fell" for that reason. What the search found on his Iran response: he refused a US request to use British bases before the initial strikes and said the UK was not involved; a joint statement with Macron and Merz on 28 February condemned Iran's counter-strikes and called for diplomacy; on 1 to 2 March he agreed to let the US use UK bases for "defensive" strikes on Iranian missiles and launchers; in the Commons he said "this government does not believe in regime change from the skies", and Kemi Badenoch replied that "the prime minister still cannot say whether he backs the strikes or not". So "wouldn't say whether the strikes were right or wrong" rests on Badenoch's charge and the absence of any criticism in the reported statements; read the Commons statement of 2 March before recording, because the "regime change from the skies" line could be read as criticism.
- **Do not use Greenland against Starmer.** In January 2026 he called Trump's Greenland tariffs "completely wrong" in a televised statement, though he ruled out retaliation. The Greenland line was cut from the script for that reason.
- **Carney's timing.** The Davos speech was in January 2026, before the strikes on Iran on 28 February. The script says "a month earlier" so it does not imply he was responding to the war.
- **Current government.** Nothing was found on Burnham's stance towards the US, so the script makes no claim about him.
- **US actions.** Tariffs on allies in April 2025 are from memory of 2025 reporting.
- **US Navy and sea lanes.** The claim that the US Navy has policed sea lanes and fought piracy since 1945 is general knowledge, from memory. "Eighty years" and "didn't matter whose ship" are simplifications.
- **Strait of Hormuz.** The US and Israel launched joint strikes on Iran on 28 February 2026; Iran closed the strait in response, and the US began a naval blockade of Iranian ports on 13 April. So Iran did the closing, after American strikes; the script says the bombs "started a war that all but shut" it and that the navy "helped close" it, which is as far as the facts go. Baseline traffic was about 85 vessels a day. Trackers disagree on current traffic: one recorded 1 transit on 1 November 2026, others about 30 a day with US coordination. The situation is moving (a headline on 10 November 2026 quotes Trump calling the war "effectively over"), so recheck on the day you record and change the tense if it has reopened. "A fifth of the world's oil" is from memory.
- **Combined economy.** UK plus EU plus Canada, Japan, Australia and South Korea being larger than the US and China is my own sum of approximate GDP figures from memory (roughly $33tn against roughly $29tn and $19tn). Recompute from current International Monetary Fund data before putting a chart on screen.
- **Joint Expeditionary Force.** NBC reported Carney announcing Canada's application to join the UK-led force in a speech to the European Parliament. The date and whether it has been accepted were not checked, so "applied" may be out of date.
