# The UN's next leader could be Viktor Orbán

Status: draft script, version 2, not yet recorded. This is a live story: recheck every fact on the day you record.
Format: talking-head thesis reel (named hook, the frontrunner, the accountability argument, British call to action), with graphics.
Target length: about 110 seconds, roughly 310 words.
Researched 12 November 2026. The APEC leaders' meeting in Shenzhen is 18 to 19 November, so this dates quickly.

## Script

The next leader of the United Nations could be Viktor Orbán.

The man Hungary voted out in April, after sixteen years. The man the European Parliament said had turned his country into an "electoral autocracy".

Or it could be Lana Nusseibeh. A government minister from the United Arab Emirates, backed by Saudi Arabia and the rest of the Gulf, and criticised by Human Rights Watch over her country's record in Sudan and Yemen.

There is a frontrunner. Rebeca Grynspan of Costa Rica. In the last vote she got twelve out of fifteen. In any normal election, that's a landslide.

It isn't enough. Because five countries can each kill any candidate on their own. America. Russia. China. Britain. France.

And the Wall Street Journal reports that Trump officials have encouraged Orbán to run, as a joint pick for America, Russia and China.

Think about what the United Nations is for. It was built in 1945 on one idea. That power answers to rules. That leaders are held to account.

Now look at who'd be choosing. Vladimir Putin is wanted by the International Criminal Court. Donald Trump put sanctions on that court. Xi Jinping's government was told by the UN's own human rights office that it may have committed crimes against humanity.

None of them wants a Secretary-General who asks questions. That's the point of picking Orbán.

But here's the part people miss. Britain has a veto. So does France. Neither has used it since 1989.

Our leaders need to say it now, out loud. If three men who answer to no one try to hand the United Nations to a fourth, we will block it.

That's what the veto is for.

## Changes from the brief, and why

- **"Dictator" became "voted out" plus a quoted description.** Orbán lost an election on 12 April 2026 and conceded the same night. Calling him a dictator invites the reply that dictators do not concede elections. "Electoral autocracy" is the European Parliament's phrase, so it is attributable and harder to dismiss.
- **Nusseibeh is Emirati, not Saudi.** She is the UAE's nominee. Saudi backing came through the Gulf Cooperation Council's unanimous endorsement.
- **The criticism is of her country's record and her role defending it**, not of human rights violations by her personally. The script says "over her country's record".
- **"For the first time in 80 years" became "since 1989".** Britain and France have both used the veto, most recently in 1989.
- **"Two dictators" became three named leaders with one sourced fact each.** The facts make the accountability point without resting on a label. "Three men who answer to no one" is your opinion and is delivered to camera as such.
- **The Orbán plan is a newspaper report, not an established fact.** He has not been nominated, and Russia's deputy foreign minister said none of the three would nominate him. The script attributes it to the Wall Street Journal. "That's the point of picking Orbán" is your reading of the motive.

## Facts to check before recording

- **Orbán.** Lost on 12 April 2026 to Péter Magyar's Tisza party (138 of 199 seats on preliminary figures) after 16 years; conceded that night. The Wall Street Journal report was 25 October. Check whether he has been nominated since, and by whom.
- **"Electoral autocracy".** From memory: a European Parliament resolution of September 2022 called Hungary a "hybrid regime of electoral autocracy". Confirm the wording and date.
- **Nusseibeh.** Appointed UAE Minister of State in September 2025; UAE ambassador to the UN 2013 to 2024; nominated 21 October 2026. Sources differ on whether she is on leave from the ministerial post or has stepped down, so "government minister" may need to become "former government minister". The Human Rights Watch criticism appeared in a search summary of coverage; a second search did not find the statement itself, so find it before using their name. The New York Times reported on her role in the UAE's denial of involvement in Sudan.
- **Grynspan.** 12 encourage, 3 discourage in the eighth informal vote on 5 November, from leaked results. Check for a later vote.
- **Putin.** International Criminal Court arrest warrant issued March 2023. From memory.
- **Trump.** Executive order imposing sanctions on International Criminal Court officials, February 2025. From memory.
- **Xi.** The UN human rights office report on Xinjiang, published 31 August 2022, said abuses "may constitute international crimes, in particular crimes against humanity". From memory; the finding is about the Chinese government, which is how the script words it.
- **The veto.** Britain and France last cast one in December 1989. From memory.
- **1945.** The Charter was signed in San Francisco in June 1945. "Built on one idea" is a simplification.
- **Timing.** Trump has said he expects to meet Xi and Putin at APEC in Shenzhen on 18 to 19 November; neither has confirmed. This version does not mention the meeting; add "and next week the three of them may be in the same room" only if it is still ahead when you record.
