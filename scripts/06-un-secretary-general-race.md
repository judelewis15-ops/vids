# Twelve votes out of fifteen, and it might not matter

Status: draft script, not yet recorded. This is a live story: recheck every fact on the day you record.
Format: talking-head thesis reel (broad verdict, the numbers, the named players, turn, British close), with graphics.
Target length: about 105 seconds, roughly 300 words.
Researched 12 November 2026. The APEC leaders' meeting in Shenzhen is 18 to 19 November, so this dates quickly.

## Script

The next leader of the United Nations might not be chosen in New York. It might be chosen next week, in a hotel in Shenzhen.

Here's how it works. Fifteen countries on the Security Council vote. You need nine. But five of them, America, Russia, China, Britain and France, can each kill any candidate on their own.

Right now, the frontrunner is Rebeca Grynspan of Costa Rica. In the last vote she got twelve out of fifteen. In any normal election, that's a landslide.

It isn't enough. Because nobody knows who the three against her are. And if one of them holds a veto, twelve votes are worth nothing.

So look at who's circling. The United Arab Emirates has put up Lana Nusseibeh, with Saudi Arabia and the rest of the Gulf behind her. Human Rights Watch has criticised her record on Sudan and Yemen.

And then there's this. The Wall Street Journal reports that Trump officials have encouraged Viktor Orbán to run. As a joint pick. For America, Russia and China.

He hasn't been nominated. Russia says it won't be the one to do it. But the big three haven't backed anyone in public. And next week, Trump says he expects to sit down with Xi and Putin.

But here's the part people miss. Britain has a veto too. So does France. Two of the five votes that matter belong to middle powers.

For eighty years that veto has mostly sat in a drawer. If three presidents try to settle the United Nations between themselves, this is what it's for.

The job has gone to a quiet compromise every time. This time, the question is whose.

## Corrections to the original brief

- The Gulf candidate is the UAE's nominee, not Saudi Arabia's. Saudi backing came through a unanimous Gulf Cooperation Council endorsement. No reporting was found of Saudi Arabia lobbying other Council members, and some analysts expected Riyadh to be wary of an Emirati in the job.
- The United States and Russia are not "holding off on giving their candidate". Permanent members do not nominate candidates; what they have not done is say publicly whom they support.
- No reporting was found that says the big three are waiting for the Shenzhen meeting to decide. The script only puts the two facts side by side ("haven't backed anyone in public" and "next week… expects to sit down"). Do not state the link as fact. One headline does say the race "could rest on US and China trade-offs".

## Facts to check before recording

- **Eighth straw poll, 5 November.** Grynspan 12 encourage, 3 discourage, 0 no opinion, per diplomats cited by The National; results are leaked, not official. Check whether a ninth poll has happened since.
- **Colour-coded ballots.** Sources conflict. Geneva Solutions and the eighth-poll reports say ballots were not yet colour-coded, so permanent members' votes are hidden; a separate headline describes a "first color-coded straw poll" in which Grynspan led with nine and "no candidate avoids a potential veto". Establish which came when. If a colour-coded poll has shown a permanent member against her, change "nobody knows" to say so.
- **Nusseibeh.** Nominated by the UAE on 21 October; Gulf Cooperation Council backing announced the next day. The New York Times reported on her role in the UAE's denial of involvement in Sudan; Human Rights Watch criticised her record on Yemen and Sudan. Her straw-poll numbers were not found.
- **Orbán.** The Wall Street Journal reported on 25 October that Trump officials encouraged him to enter as a possible joint US, Russia and China pick. He is described as the former Hungarian prime minister, has expressed interest, and has not been nominated. Russia's deputy foreign minister said none of the three will nominate him, on the grounds that permanent members do not nominate. Poland's foreign minister does not expect to support him. One report said any announcement was being held until after the US midterms. Check whether anyone has nominated him since.
- **Shenzhen.** APEC leaders' meeting 18 to 19 November. Trump has said he expects to meet Xi and Putin; neither has confirmed. "A hotel" is colour, not reporting.
- **Other candidates.** Carolyn Rodrigues-Birkett of Guyana has been a main contender; Türkiye's Feridun Sinirlioğlu entered on 1 November and got 3 encourage, 8 discourage. The South China Morning Post reports China is considering Grynspan and Rodrigues-Birkett and has ruled out Rafael Grossi. The script leaves these out for length.
- **Deadline.** Guterres's term ends 31 December 2026.
- **The veto "in a drawer".** From memory: Britain and France have not cast a veto since 1989. Confirm before using "eighty years" and "mostly".
- **"A quiet compromise every time"** is my characterisation, not a sourced claim.
